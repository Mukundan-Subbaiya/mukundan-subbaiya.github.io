Brief & RESULT

Load-Application Methods for a Pin-Loaded Cantilever Beam

ANSYS MechanicalNonlinear ContactMesh Convergence

Three different ways to apply the same physical load in FEA give three different answers — and only a convergence study reveals which ones are trustworthy and which are an artifact of the modelling choice itself.

Setup

A load applied through a pin-in-hole joint on a cantilever beam was modelled three ways: Remote Force (four internal distribution variants), a cosine-distributed Bearing Load, and an explicit, nonlinearly-solved Contact model (with and without friction). Each method's global deformation and local stress were checked across a mesh-refinement sweep.

Validation

Global deformation converged smoothly and consistently to the same value (≈0.92–0.93 mm) across all three methods with mesh refinement — confirming the overall structural response is insensitive to the load-modelling choice. Peak local stress at the hole boundary was checked separately, since that's where the methods actually diverge.

Result

The Coupled and Rigid Remote Force variants produce an artificial kinematic constraint at the hole that never lets the local stress converge — it keeps climbing with every mesh refinement because it's a modelling artifact, not physics. The Deformable, Bearing Load, and Contact methods all converge to consistent, physically realistic stress fields instead. The practical conclusion: for local stress accuracy near a loaded joint, Coupled/Rigid Remote Force is the wrong tool, regardless of mesh quality.
