Brief & RESULT

Transient Thermo-Mechanical Analysis of a Gas Turbine Disc

ANSYS MechanicalCoupled Thermal–Structural FEM

A steady-state stress check on a turbine disc can pass comfortably — and still miss the moment the disc is actually closest to failure.

Setup

A SIEMENS GT35C high-pressure turbine disc was modelled from cold start to a fully warmed-up state at 3000 s, at its 7000 RPM operating speed. The analysis progressed through three geometric approximation levels — constant thickness, tapered, and a detailed fillet geometry — then combined the transient temperature field with centrifugal and blade-pull mechanical loading via quasi-static superposition.

Validation

Before touching the real geometry, the constant-thickness FE model was checked against a closed-form analytical solution for combined centrifugal and blade-pull hoop stress — agreeing to within 1.17%, which confirmed the FE setup, boundary conditions, and material properties before adding thermal complexity.

Result

Mechanical loading alone gives a peak bore stress of 478 MPa at steady state. Add the thermal gradient from the hot gas path and that rises to 585 MPa — the hotter outer rim wants to expand, the cooler bore resists it, and that 'clamping' effect adds directly to the centrifugal stress rather than partially cancelling it. The real headline, though, is transient: during the start-up ramp the stress overshoots even the steady combined value, meaning a steady-state-only check would have missed the disc's actual worst moment.
