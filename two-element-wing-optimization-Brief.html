PROJECT BRIEF

DoE–RSM–MOGA Optimization of a Two-Element Downforce Wing

ANSYS DesignXplorerMOGAResponse Surface

A full DoE–response-surface–optimization workflow applied to a two-element inverted wing at 70 km/h — not a parametric sweep, but a surrogate model searched by a genetic algorithm and then checked against a real simulation.

Setup

Three parameters — main-element angle of attack, secondary-element angle of attack, and the vertical gap between elements — were varied using a 15-point Optimal Space-Filling DoE in ANSYS DesignXplorer. A Genetic Aggregation response surface was fitted to the CL and CD outputs, then searched with a Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm (MOGA) to find the configuration maximizing downforce at minimum drag.

Validation

The CL response surface reached a cross-validation R² of 0.9995. The MOGA-recommended optimum wasn't taken on faith — it was re-run as a direct CFD simulation, which matched the surrogate model's prediction to within 0.88% on CL and 6.1% on CD.

Result

The optimizer converged on a higher angle of attack on both elements and a 50% wider slot gap than the baseline. That combination increases bound circulation on the main element and re-energizes the boundary layer on the secondary element via a stronger slot jet — delivering 54.2% more downforce and a 21.4% better lift-to-drag ratio, at the cost of 27% more drag.
