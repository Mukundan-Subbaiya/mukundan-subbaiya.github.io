GIST & RESULT

Turbulence Model Benchmarking on a Simplified Cybertruck Geometry

ANSYS Fluentk–ε vs k–ω SSTRe ≈ 588,000

Two RANS turbulence models, one simplified bluff-body EV geometry, and a direct question: which model actually earns its keep for early-stage aerodynamic design decisions?

Setup

A simplified Cybertruck-like geometry was simulated in steady RANS at a Reynolds number of 588,000 (5 m/s highway-equivalent). Two turbulence models were meshed and run separately — standard k–ε with a ~900,000-node mesh, and k–ω SST with a finer near-wall mesh (y⁺ ≈ 1). Mesh independence was confirmed to within 3% for both.

Validation

Both models were checked against reference drag and lift data along the vehicle's length, not just as single bulk numbers — this is what exposes where a model actually breaks down rather than averaging the error away.

Result

Both models tracked the reference drag buildup well over the front and mid-body, then systematically overpredicted drag in the separated rear wake — k–ω SST reached 14% error, k–ε reached 20%. Both underpredicted the lift peak over the slanted roof by 20–40%, a known steady-RANS limitation where unsteady vortex shedding isn't captured. SST's blended near-wall/freestream formulation made it the clear choice for the follow-on design study.
