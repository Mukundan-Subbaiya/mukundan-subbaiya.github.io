Brief & RESULT

Large Eddy Simulation of Periodic Hill Flow

ANSYS FluentWALE LESICEM CFDRe_H = 10,595

A wall-resolved LES, verified and validated end to end, then used to answer a real siting question from the wall-shear-stress field — not stopped at a validation plot.

Setup

A fully structured 0.98-million-cell hexahedral mesh was built in ANSYS ICEM CFD for the classic periodic-hill benchmark (ReH = 10,595). A precursor steady k–ω SST RANS run first characterized the flow and set the mesh/time-step requirements, before a wall-resolved LES (WALE subgrid model, y⁺ < 0.3) was run in ANSYS Fluent to capture the unsteady separated shear layer directly.

Validation

Before trusting any result, the LES was verified on its own terms — near-wall and bulk-flow resolution, temporal (Courant number) resolution, subgrid-model activity, and two-point spatial correlations — then validated against the DNS reference data of Krank et al. across mean velocity, Reynolds stress, turbulent kinetic energy, and the bottom-wall skin-friction and pressure distributions shown above.

Result

The LES cut the recirculation-length error against DNS to 37.6%, versus 70.4% for the precursor RANS — roughly half the error, from the same mesh. That validated wall-shear-stress field was then used for something beyond the CFD exercise itself: identifying a specific region of the valley (5.5 ≤ x/H ≤ 6.5) with the lowest, most directionally stable wall loading, as a physical siting recommendation.
