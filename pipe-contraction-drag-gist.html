Brief & RESULT

Drag Reduction Through a Sudden Pipe Contraction

ANSYS CFXLaminar CFDDoE Parametric Study

A validated CFD model of a classic pipe-contraction problem, then a passive drag-reduction idea (a small wall-mounted obstacle) tested against the model rather than assumed to work.

Setup

Laminar flow through a sudden pipe contraction (ReD = 398–1213) was modelled in ANSYS CFX, matching the classic experimental configuration of Durst & Loy (1985). A three-grid mesh verification study (Celik et al. procedure) was run first, at the highest Reynolds number in the range, to quantify discretization uncertainty before trusting any result from the model.

Validation

The fine-grid Grid Convergence Index came out to 0.88% for the vena-contracta area and 1.86% for the pressure-loss coefficient — low enough to trust the model. The CFD results were then checked directly against Durst & Loy's measured data across the full Reynolds-number range, reproducing the correct decreasing trend with only minor deviation at the highest Re, where transitional effects begin to matter.

Result

With the baseline model trusted, a small wall-mounted obstacle was added upstream of the contraction and its height, width, and position swept via a DoE study. The optimized obstacle placement cut the pressure-loss coefficient by up to 6.64% at the highest Reynolds number — a reduction more than 3.5× larger than the model's own discretization uncertainty, meaning it's a real effect, not numerical noise.
