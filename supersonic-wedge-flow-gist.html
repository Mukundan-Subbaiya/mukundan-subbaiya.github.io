Brief & RESULT

Supersonic Wedge Flow — Oblique Shock Validation

ANSYS FluentDensity-Based EulerM∞ = 2, 3, 4

Compressible CFD checked against the exact textbook physics it's supposed to reproduce — oblique shock theory — across three Mach numbers and a sweep of wedge angles.

Setup

A density-based Euler CFD solver in ANSYS Fluent modelled supersonic flow over a wedge at three freestream Mach numbers, sweeping the wedge deflection angle at each. A reproducible plateau-extraction method was used to read shock angle and post-shock flow properties directly from the CFD solution.

Validation

Every CFD data point was checked against the analytical θ–β–M relation and the corresponding oblique-shock property ratios — not just at one condition, but across the full attached-shock range at each Mach number, including the maximum deflection angle before the shock detaches.

Result

Across all tested conditions, the CFD results tracked the analytical theory closely on every measured quantity: shock angle, density ratio, static pressure ratio, and static temperature ratio. This kind of systematic, multi-condition agreement is what actually earns confidence in a compressible-flow solver setup, rather than a single spot-check.

<1.5%pressure-ratio error vs. theory
<2%temperature-ratio error vs. theory
<3%shock-angle / density-ratio error vs. theory
